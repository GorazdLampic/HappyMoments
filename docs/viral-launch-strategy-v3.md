## 1. THE REAL VALUE PROPOSITION

### What the app IS

**HappyMoments is a relationship tool disguised as a number calculator.**

It gives you a reason to text someone today. Not a generic "hey, how are you?" but a specific, personal, surprising reason: "Did you know our friendship just turned 5,000 days? That happened last Tuesday."

The numbers are the excuse. The celebration is the point. The reconnection is the real value.

### Two core use cases

| Use Case | Example | Emotional Driver |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| **Celebrate** | "Your daughter turns 5,000 days old next Tuesday!" | Joy, parental pride, marking time |
| **Reconnect** | "Hey, I just found out you turn 15,000 days next week!" | Longing, friendship, excuse to reach out |

The reconnection use case is just as important as celebration. Many people have someone they have been meaning to text — an old friend, an ex-colleague, a family member they lost touch with. HappyMoments gives them the perfect, non-awkward excuse: a specific, personal, time-limited reason to reach out. This is not a forced "just checking in." It is a genuine discovery worth sharing.

### The value proposition (one sentence)

> **"Discover every reason to celebrate with the people you care about."**

### Supporting statements (for different contexts)

| Context | Statement |
|---------|-----------|
| For the app store | "Discover hidden reasons to celebrate with your people." |
| For a friend | "It gives you a personal reason to text someone today." |
| For social media | "The app that turns ordinary days into celebrations." |
| For parents | "Know when your child hits 5,000 days, 1 million minutes, and 50 other milestones." |
| For couples | "520 days together means 'I love you' in Chinese. When's yours?" |
| For reconnectors | "The perfect excuse to reach out to someone you've been meaning to text." |
| For cultural communities | "Every culture has sacred and lucky numbers. The app knows when you hit them." |

**Language principle:** All messaging uses direct positive statements. Avoid constructions that negate a negative ("never miss," "don't let it pass," "won't forget"). Instead, frame everything as discovery, celebration, and action ("find every reason," "celebrate every moment," "discover what's coming").

### Why this framing works

The framing "I have a reason to reach out to SOMEONE" creates a habit loop. Every time the app surfaces a milestone for someone you care about, you have a reason to act. That act — whether a celebration or a reconnection — strengthens a relationship. That emotional reward brings you back.

You return because the app told you your mother turns 25,000 days old next week, and that is a reason to call her. Or because your university friend hits 15,000 days and you have been meaning to reconnect anyway — now you have the perfect opening.

## 2. THE HOOKS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

Every age group has their own "wow" moment. The billion-seconds hook only lands for people aged 27-35. Here is the full map.

**Important practical note:** When people share content, we cannot control who sees it. So hooks need to be either universal or self-selecting. The best hooks work because the recipient immediately recognizes whether it applies to them: "Your kid turns 5,000 days on Friday" works for any parent regardless of age. We do not rely on precisely targeted messaging — instead, we make the shared content naturally appealing so that the right people self-select.

**Their hooks:**

The most viral hooks are those that work regardless of who sees them. These are the priority for shared content:

| Hook | Why It Self-Selects |

| "Text someone you care about — their next milestone is closer than you think" | Universal reconnection impulse |
| "Your family's combined age just crossed [big number]" | Any family can calculate this |

These self-selecting hooks are what we prioritize for group shares. The recipient decides if it applies to them — we do not need to target.

### 3.8 Palindrome Quality Standards

When using palindrome examples in any content, only use "pretty" palindromes — those with internal structure and visual elegance. Ugly or arbitrary palindromes diminish the wow factor.

**Good palindrome examples (use these):**

| Palindrome | Why It's Good |
|------------|---------------|
| 12,321 | Ascending then descending — clean staircase pattern |
| 45,654 | Same staircase, higher range |
| 1,234,321 | Full staircase — very satisfying |
| 10,001 | Clean symmetry, round feel |
| 11,211 | Repeating pattern |
| 98,789 | Descending then ascending — reverse staircase |

**Avoid using:**
- Palindromes that look random (e.g., 56,065 — no clear internal pattern)
- Very short palindromes that feel trivial (e.g., 121)
- Palindromes where the pattern is not immediately visually obvious

**Rule:** Before publishing any palindrome content, verify the number has a visually appealing internal pattern. If in doubt, pick a different number type (round number, Fibonacci, cultural).

## 5. THE VIRAL LOOP — BUILT AROUND GROUPS

### 5.1 Why Groups Are the Key to k > 1

Individual 1:1 shares are valuable but linear: one person shares to one person, who may or may not share onward. The viral coefficient (k-factor) from 1:1 sharing is typically 0.1-0.3 — not enough for self-sustaining growth.

**Groups change the math entirely:**

| Mechanic | Reach | Conversion | Result |
|----------|-------|-----------|--------|
| 1:1 share | 1 person | ~30% install | 0.3 new users per share |
| WhatsApp group (25 people) | 25 people | ~10% install | 2.5 new users per share |
| Viber group (20 people) | 20 people | ~10% install | 2.0 new users per share |
| Instagram group chat (10 people) | 10 people | ~8% install | 0.8 new users per share |
| Facebook group post (100+ people) | 100+ people | ~3% install | 3.0+ new users per share |

**The "Challenge Your Group" button is the primary viral mechanic.** It shares a pre-composed message to a group chat with one tap:

```
"I just discovered my next milestone: [X]. What's YOURS?
Check your milestones: happymoments.app"
```

### 5.2 The Group Viral Loop

```
User discovers milestone
    |
    v
Taps "Challenge Your Group" --> sends to WhatsApp/Viber/Instagram group
    |
    v
Group members see the challenge (20-50 people per group)
    |
    v
3-5 people respond: enter THEIR birthday, discover THEIR milestones
    |
    v
1-2 of those people challenge THEIR groups
    |
    v
[REPEAT — branching chain]
```

**The math:**

```
User A --> 1 group (30 people) --> 4 install --> 2 challenge THEIR groups
    Each new group (25 people) --> 3 install --> 1 challenges another group
        Each new group (25 people) --> 2 install --> ...
```

Even with modest conversion (10% install from group, 20% of installers challenge another group), each seed user generates 1.5-2.5 additional users over 3 "generations." If the challenge rate reaches 30%, the loop becomes self-sustaining (k > 1).

### 5.3 Group-Optimized Share Messages

Every share message must be optimized for group contexts — meaning it should be interesting to EVERYONE in the group, not just one person:

| Share Type | Message Template | Group Appeal |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Challenge | "I just found out I turn [X] days old on [date]. That's a [palindrome/Fibonacci/lucky number]. What's YOUR next cool milestone? happymoments.app" | Everyone wants to check theirs |
| Family group | "Our family's combined age just crossed [X] days! Check what milestones are coming for our family: happymoments.app" | Every family member is included |
| Friend group | "Which one of us has the most interesting upcoming milestone? Find yours: happymoments.app" | Competition drives engagement |
| Parent group | "My kid turns 5,000 days next week. When does yours hit a cool number? happymoments.app" | Parent comparison is irresistible |
| Reconnection | "I was playing with this app and found out you turn [milestone] soon. Just wanted to say hi! happymoments.app" | Works even 1:1 within a group context |

## 7. THE ONBOARDING

### The Flow

```
Welcome: "What would you like to discover?"

→ Option A (DEFAULT, prominent): "My own milestones" → Enter own birthday
    → Show YOUR milestones → "Here's what's coming up for you"
    → WOW moment: hero card with best upcoming milestone
    → Prompt: "Share this with someone" or "Challenge your group"
    → THEN: "Now add someone you care about. See what you have in common."

→ Option B (secondary, smaller): "Someone else's milestones" → Enter THEIR name + birthday
    → Show THEIR milestones → "Here's what's coming up for [name]"
    → Prompt: "Send [name] their next milestone"
    → THEN: "Now add yourself. See what you have in common."
```

### Why "Me First" is the Default

Most people do not know the exact birthday of friends off the top of their head. And even if they do, there might be no impressive milestone coming soon for that person — which kills the first impression.

With your own birthday:
1. You always know the exact date
2. There is always something interesting coming up (the app guarantees this with many milestone types)
3. The "aha" moment is personal and immediate
4. AFTER experiencing the aha, you are much more motivated to enter someone else's birthday

The "celebrate someone" angle is powerful — but it works better as the SECOND action, once the user already understands and trusts the app.

Option B ("Someone else's milestones") remains available for users who arrive with a specific intent: "I want to check when my kid hits 5,000 days." These users already know the birthday and have high intent — they will have a great experience either way.

### Onboarding for Deep-Link Recipients

When someone clicks a shared link:

```
See FRIEND'S milestones instantly (zero friction)
    → "When's YOUR special number?"
    → Enter own birthday → See YOUR milestones
    → "You and [friend] together: [combined milestone]!"
    → Prompt to share, add more people, challenge your group
```

This is already built. It is the most powerful onboarding path because the user arrives with social proof (a friend sent them this) and curiosity (what are MY milestones?).

### First-Session Checklist

For maximum activation, the first session should achieve:

1. Enter at least one person's birthday (theirs or someone else's) -- YES if they completed onboarding
2. See at least one "wow" milestone -- YES (hero card)
3. Take ONE social action (share, send, or challenge) -- this is the gap to optimize

The gap is #3. Currently ~10-20% of users share in their first session. Target: >30%. Making "Me First" the default ensures the strongest possible first impression, which increases the likelihood of sharing. The "Challenge Your Group" button immediately after the hero card is the key conversion point.

## 8. THE WEEKLY/MONTHLY RHYTHM

What keeps the app relevant over time? Not push notifications alone (those are reminders). The app needs to provide ongoing VALUE that makes people want to check it.

### Daily

**In-app:** "Today's Number" — a historical event with a nice number. This changes every day and is interesting even without any personal milestones. It is ambient content that rewards opening the app.

**Social media:** One post using the day's nice number (AI-generated on Sunday, scheduled for the week).

**Push notification (if upcoming milestones exist):** "[Name] turns [milestone] in 3 days!" — only when there is something real to celebrate. Or: "[Name]'s milestone is coming up — the perfect time to reach out."

### Weekly

| Day | What | Channel |
|-----|------|---------|
| Monday | "Milestone of the Week" — spotlight one upcoming milestone type | Social media + in-app banner |
| Wednesday | Mid-week check: "This week's milestones for your people" | Push notification (if applicable) |
| Friday | "Weekend milestones" — what's happening Saturday/Sunday | Push notification + social post |
| Sunday | Batch session: Claude generates, Gorazd reviews and schedules | Internal workflow |

### Monthly

- **"Month in Numbers" summary:** "This month, 3 milestones happened for your people. Here's what's coming next month."
- **New historical events added:** Keep the Daily Nice Numbers calendar fresh
- **Seasonal angle:** Tie to holidays, seasons, cultural events

### Quarterly / Seasonal

| Period | Theme | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| New Year (Jan) | "Your Year in Numbers" | Spotify Wrapped equivalent: all milestones in the coming year, shareable card |
| Valentine's (Feb) | "Love Numbers" | 520, 1314, combined couple milestones, "days we've been in love" |
| Spring | "New Beginnings" | Baby milestones, new relationships, fresh starts |
| Summer | "Family Milestones" | Kids' summer birthdays, family trips as milestone markers |
| Back to School (Sep) | "Friendship Numbers" | School friendships in days, teacher milestones |
| Holidays (Nov-Dec) | "Year-End Celebration" | "Your Year in Numbers" card (the big annual feature), gift store push |

### The "Year in Numbers" Card (Annual Feature)

This is the single most important retention and viral feature for the future. Like Spotify Wrapped, it creates:

1. **Anticipation:** Everyone's sharing theirs, you want yours too
2. **Social currency:** Your numbers are unique, interesting, shareable
3. **Annual habit:** Users return every December/January
4. **Data richness:** The more people you've added, the richer your card

**Card contents:**
- Total milestones that happened this year for all your people
- The most interesting milestone of the year
- Combined milestones for your groups
- Cultural milestones hit this year
- Preview: the biggest milestone coming next year
- "You celebrated with [X] people this year"

**Implementation timing:** Build for December 2026/January 2027. Announce early ("Your Year in Numbers is coming in December — add your people now to get the full picture").

## 9. THE GIFT STORE — BEHAVIORAL PERSONALIZATION (Future)

### 9.1 Basic Gift Store

The gift store offers physical products (mugs, prints, cards) tied to milestones. This is already planned.

### 9.2 Behavioral Signal-Based Suggestions

Beyond basic milestone-triggered suggestions, the app can use behavioral signals to recommend relevant gifts. This is future work but belongs in the strategy because it makes the gift store significantly more effective.

**Signal-to-suggestion mapping:**

| User Behavior Signal | What It Suggests | Gift Recommendation |
|---------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Shares morning-time milestones, coffee-related history facts | Coffee/morning person | Custom milestone mugs, "10,000 mornings" coffee mug |
| Adds business contacts, tracks professional anniversaries | Professional context | Premium milestone cards, desk items, professional prints |
| Tracks children's milestones, shares to family groups | Parent/family-oriented | Kids' milestone posters, family combined-age prints, cute items |
| Tracks couple milestones, uses 520/1314 features | Romantic context | Couple milestone prints, "520 days" cards, jewelry-adjacent items |

| Tracks many people (10+), frequent sharer | Social connector | Bulk card packs, group milestone posters, party supplies |
| Tracks grandchildren | Grandparent | Large-text prints, photo-milestone frames, family tree items |

**Implementation approach:**
1. Track which milestone types and history facts users engage with most
2. Build a lightweight preference profile (stored locally, privacy-first)
3. Surface relevant gift suggestions when a milestone is approaching: "Your daughter turns 5,000 days next week. Here's a 5,000-day poster for her room."
4. Start simple: 3 signal types (parent, couple, professional) in v1 of the gift store

**Privacy principle:** All signal processing happens on-device. No behavioral data leaves the phone. Suggestions are generated locally from the user's own data.
