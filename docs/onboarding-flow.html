Design Principles

  1. Every screen delivers a surprise. No screen is "setup."
  2. User never does work without immediate reward. Enter date → instant wow.
  3. Share is the natural next step, not a request.
  4. Guide the user on the exact desired path (Luka Renko: "moraš voditi uporabnika po točno želeni poti").
  5. After each input, a feeling of satisfaction — something happens as a reward. Never feel like accounting software.

The Journey: Group-Based Onboarding

v2.0 — 9-screen progressive reveal. User discovers their milestones first, then adds ONE person, then builds a group:

1. Your date — DD/MM/YYYY, auto-advance on day≥4 and month≥2
2. Your hero reveal — counter animation, no cosmic milestones
3. Your milestones list — 8 milestones, 730-day window, all tappable to share
4. Add ONE person — name text input + role hint chips (Partner/Mom/Dad/Sister/Brother)
5. Their hero reveal + milestones — merged on one screen, list hidden until animation finishes, all tappable
6. Combined milestones + group name — full engine across all time units (palindromes, repdigits, Pi, powers of 2), shows 6 combined, group name auto-suggested
7. Group builder — name+date on one row, growing member list, "See milestones" at 2+ members
8. Group reveal — group combined + individual milestones for NEW members only, pencil to edit, all tappable
9. Share screen — each person's milestone with preview, then "Go to my dashboard"

Key rules:
• Back buttons on all screens (3-9). Resume banner if onboarding interrupted early.
• Share flow on milestone screens: tap milestone row → gold highlight (ms-selected class) + floating "Share →" bubble at bottom center → native share picker.
• autocomplete="off" on all name inputs to suppress password managers.
• Cosmic milestones: max 1 globally, description only (no redundant number). If the hero reveal shows a cosmic milestone, it also appears in the milestone list below for consistency.
• On finish, user lands on Me tab with 3 bottom tabs (Me | Together | Edit).

Elements: Inspirational examples + "What about you?" + DD/MM/YYYY input

User action: Read the examples (2 seconds), then type birthday. Auto-advance triggers when day≥4 and month≥2.

Reward: Curiosity — "what's MY number?"

Psychology: The examples show OTHER people's milestones first. This creates social proof + curiosity ("she has a billionth-second birthday — what about me?"). Value before identity (Duolingo principle).

Transition: Auto-advance or "Show me" button → counter animation starts → Screen 2

Why examples first: Without examples, "billionth-second birthday" is abstract. With examples, the user immediately sees: (1) these are real milestones people have, (2) they're sharable moments ("she just turned..."), (3) they cover different scales (billions of seconds, thousands of days). The examples ARE the elevator pitch.
What is NOT here: App name. Logo. Tagline. Language picker. Consent banner. Name field. No explanation of what the app does — the examples show it.

v2.0 flow — gradual reveal, always forward:
The journey builds: (1) YOUR hero → (2) YOUR milestone list → (3) add ONE person → (4) THEIR hero + milestones → (5) combined milestones → (6) group builder → (7) group reveal → (8) share screen → dashboard. Share available at every milestone screen (tap row → gold highlight ms-selected + floating "Share →" bubble at bottom center). Back buttons on screens 3-9.

The number: Counter rolls from 0 to target (1.5–2s, ease-out-cubic). Gold glow on completion. Sparkle particles.

Emotional countdown rules:

  • Past, yesterday: "That was yesterday!"
  • Past, recent: "You passed it X ago"
  • Future, ≤7 days: "That's this week!"
  • Future, ≤30 days: "Coming in just X days"
  • Future, >30 days: "X months from now"

Milestone selection for reveal: Balance impressiveness × proximity. Prefer:

  1. Birthday (upcoming)
  2. Big milestones (billion seconds, 10K days) — highest wow factor
  3. Round thousands in days
  4. Saturn return
  5. Avoid: Fibonacci, palindromes, obscure patterns for the FIRST reveal
Past vs. near future vs. far future — critical design difference:

The reveal must feel exciting regardless of WHEN the milestone falls. Three scenarios:

Milestone is in the PAST (e.g., billion seconds was 1 year ago):
→ Show it as a discovery: "You passed your billionth second on March 23, 2025!" The value is learning it happened. CTA: "Now someone I love →" (find THEIR upcoming milestone)

Milestone is NEAR FUTURE (≤30 days):
→ Maximum excitement: "That's in 12 days!" The countdown IS the reward. CTA: "Now someone I love →" (share the excitement)

Milestone is FAR FUTURE (>6 months):
→ The algorithm should prefer a CLOSER milestone from a different unit. If the billion seconds is 4 years away, show 12,000 days instead (if it's 3 months away). Only show a far-future milestone if it's truly the most impressive option (1 billion seconds always wins over 12,321 days). Frame it as anticipation: "Coming in 14 months — mark your calendar."

Rule: Never show a milestone that's both unimpressive AND far away. A Fibonacci number 2 years from now is the worst possible onboarding reveal.

Elements: Each person's milestone with share preview text. "Go to my dashboard" button. If only 1 group exists, "Add another group" secondary button. Tab guide text below: Me — personal milestones · Together — group milestones · Edit — manage groups & people.

User action: Review share previews, optionally tap to share individual milestones, then proceed to dashboard.

Reward: Ready-to-send messages create immediate sharing opportunity. The tab guide text prepares the user for what they'll find on each tab.

Transition: "Go to my dashboard →" deposits user into the Me tab with 3 bottom tabs (Me | Together | Edit).

The dashboard IS the daily app. Onboarding deposits the user into exactly the state they'll see every day. No disorientation. The time-chunked milestone list, person filter, and 3 bottom tabs are the daily experience from now on. No hero card in daily use — all milestones are equal.

Skip Paths

Back buttons are available on screens 3-9, allowing the user to go back to any previous screen. If onboarding is interrupted (app closed), a resume banner appears on next open to continue where they left off.

Screens 1 and 2 are mandatory (you can't skip entering YOUR birthday — that IS the product). The flow is linear: each screen leads to the next.

Implementation Notes

State management

  • Screen 1: saves date to localStorage on auto-advance or "Show me" tap
  • Screen 4: saves second person to appData on submission
  • Screen 6: saves combined milestone data + group name
  • Screen 7: saves additional group members
  • Screen 9: sets hm_onboarded = 1 → next app open goes straight to Me tab
  • Interruption: resume state saved at each screen transition. Resume banner on next open.

Analytics events

  • onboard_date_entered (Screen 1 → 2)
  • onboard_reveal_seen (Screen 2 viewed)
  • onboard_milestones_browsed (Screen 3 viewed)
  • onboard_add_person (Screen 4 submitted)
  • onboard_their_reveal (Screen 5 viewed)
  • onboard_combined_seen (Screen 6 viewed)
  • onboard_group_built (Screen 7 → 8)
  • onboard_share_initiated (any screen, milestone tapped to share)
  • onboard_complete (Screen 9 → dashboard)

Hidden elements during onboarding

  • Main app header (shown after onboarding)
  • Language picker (shown after onboarding, auto-detected from browser)
  • Tab navigation (shown after Screen 9, Me tab is default)
  • Consent banner (auto-accepted on first tap)

Input behavior

  • autocomplete="off" on all name inputs to suppress password managers
  • Date input: DD/MM/YYYY format, auto-advance when day≥4 and month≥2
  • Role hint chips on Screen 4: Partner/Mom/Dad/Sister/Brother (tap to pre-fill name)
