Part 2: Top 15 UX Principles (Ranked by Impact for HappyMoments)

#PrincipleSourceReal-World ExampleWhat It Means for UsPriority
1 Value before identity
Deliver the product’s value before asking for ANY user data		 Duolingo, Headspace, TikTok Duolingo: play a lesson before signing up (+20% retention) Show milestones before asking for name, email, or preferences. Currently we ask birthday first — that’s the minimum. Never add more. CRITICAL
2 One thing per screen
Each screen has ONE primary action		 Luke Wroblewski, Apple HIG Cash App: amount entry is the ONLY thing on screen Our dashboard has ~12 competing elements. Hero milestone should dominate. Everything else is secondary. CRITICAL
3 Peak-End Rule
People judge an experience by its peak moment and its ending		 Daniel Kahneman (Nobel laureate) Spotify Wrapped: the “top artist” reveal IS the product The milestone reveal (counter animation + date) is our peak. It needs to be spectacular. The end should be a share action, not a confusing dashboard. CRITICAL
4 Design the shareable artifact first
If it can’t be screenshotted and posted, it won’t go viral		 Spotify Wrapped team (documented principle) Wrapped: every card is 9:16, high contrast, screenshot-ready Our share is text-only. We need beautiful, Instagram-ready milestone cards as the primary share format. HIGH
5 Progressive disclosure
Show complexity only when the user asks for it		 Don Norman, Apple HIG iPhone Settings: top level is simple, depth is optional Number type preferences, advanced filters, gift store — all should be hidden until needed. Default: show the best milestones, all categories on. HIGH
6 Hick’s Law
Decision time increases with the number of choices		 Jon Yablonski / lawsofux.com Google homepage: one input, one button 4 tabs + person filter + highlights toggle + add button = too many choices on the main screen. Reduce to: hero card + scrollable list + add. HIGH
7 Emotional framing beats raw data
Numbers must tell a story, not just display facts		 Spotify Wrapped, Aarron Walter “788 hours finding yourself” not “47,283 minutes” “10,000 days” should say “A life milestone — like turning 18, but in days.” Add context, not just the number. HIGH
8 Hook Model
Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment		 Nir Eyal Instagram: notification (trigger) → open (action) → new content (reward) → post/like (investment) Trigger: push notification “Mom turns 20,000 days tomorrow”. Action: open app. Reward: see the milestone card. Investment: share it to her. MEDIUM
9 Fitts’s Law
Bigger targets + closer to thumb = easier to hit		 Paul Fitts (1954), all mobile guidelines iOS: bottom tab bar in the thumb zone Our CTA buttons are OK (full-width). But the share icon on each milestone card is tiny. Make share more accessible. MEDIUM
10 Von Restorff Effect
The thing that stands out is remembered		 Hedwig von Restorff (1933) Spotify: green “Play” button stands out from gray UI Our hero milestone should be the ONLY gold element. Everything else gray. Currently too many things compete for attention with gold accents. MEDIUM
11 Typography hierarchy: max 3 levels
Hero, body, caption. Nothing else.		 Apple HIG, Material Design Apple Weather: temperature is huge, details are small, nothing in between We use 8 font sizes. Should be 3: the number (huge), the explanation (readable), metadata (small). That’s it. WORTH IT
12 Doherty Threshold
Response time must be under 400ms or users lose engagement		 Walter Doherty (IBM Research, 1982) Google Instant Search: results appear as you type Our milestone calculation is fast. But the counter animation (1.5-2s) is right — it builds anticipation without feeling slow. OK AS-IS
13 Accessibility: 16px minimum body text
Never go below 16px (1rem) for body text on mobile		 Apple HIG (17pt), Material Design (16sp), WCAG Every major app: body text is 16-17px We just fixed this — raised our type scale floor. But our tabs and badges still go to 14px (xs). Acceptable for labels, not for content. JUST FIXED
14 Miller’s Law
People can hold 7±2 items in working memory		 George Miller (1956) Phone numbers: chunked into groups of 3-4 Our milestone list shows 20-50 items. Should chunk: “This week” / “This month” / “Later this year” — max 5-7 per chunk. WORTH IT
15 Jakob’s Law
Users expect your app to work like the apps they already use		 Jakob Nielsen Every social app: bottom tab bar, pull-to-refresh, swipe gestures Our tab nav looks custom. Should match iOS/Android conventions more closely. Share flow should use native share sheet exclusively. LATER

Part 6: Next Steps (Discussion Points)

These are the decisions we need to make together before implementing:

  1. Dashboard simplification — Do we collapse 4 tabs to 2 (Personal + Settings)? Or keep 4 but make Personal the dominant 90% experience?
  2. Sharing format — The image card generator (imageCard.js) already exists. Do we make it the DEFAULT share method instead of text?
  3. Milestone framing — Add one-line explanations like “A life milestone — like turning 18, but in days”? Or keep just the number and let it speak?
  4. What to kill now vs later — History facts, gift store, highlights toggle — remove now for beta? Or keep and hide?
  5. “Your Life in Numbers” story format — Build a Wrapped-style swipeable experience? This is high effort but potentially highest viral impact.
  6. Widget — Worth building for the beta, or post-launch?
The one-sentence strategy: Make the daily experience as simple as Calm (one hero, one action), make the peak moment as spectacular as Spotify Wrapped (progressive reveal), and make sharing as easy as Instagram (one-tap beautiful card).
